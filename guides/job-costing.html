Job Costing for Small Contractors: A Simple System That Actually Gets Used

6-minute read · Running the business

Job costing just means tracking what each job actually cost you and comparing it to what you estimated. Big companies use expensive software for it. A small contractor can do it with a job number and ten minutes a week.

Why bother?

  • Find your money-losers. Many contractors are surprised by which job types make the most and least.
  • Fix your estimates. If labor runs over on every bathroom, your bathroom estimates are wrong.
  • Price with confidence. Real numbers from past jobs beat guessing.

The 4-step system

1. Give every job a number

Something simple like J-1001, J-1002. Put it on the estimate, the invoice and every receipt.

2. Tag every cost with the job number

Write it on paper receipts, add it to the memo on card purchases, and note it on payroll and sub payments. Costs that don't belong to a job (insurance, phone, ads) are overhead and get no job number.

3. Sort costs into a few categories

CategoryExamples
MaterialsLumber, fixtures, fasteners, paint
LaborYour crew's wages for that job
SubcontractorsElectrician, excavation, drywall sub
Other job costsPermits, dumpster, equipment rental

4. Compare estimate vs. actual when the job closes

Example deck jobEstimatedActual
Materials$5,675$6,467
Labor$1,680$2,020
Subs$450$450
Other$660$660
Total cost$8,465$9,597
Margin (on $11,956 revenue incl. change order)~25% planned19.7%

In this example the job was still profitable, but materials ran 14% over. The fix is a better material takeoff or a higher material markup on the next deck.

Numbers to review every month

  • Gross margin by job: which jobs hit your target and which didn't
  • Jobs over estimate: how many, and in which category
  • Money owed to you: unpaid balances on finished jobs
  • Overhead: costs with no job number, which your margins must cover

Bonus: it makes tax time easier

If you're logging every cost with a category anyway, you're most of the way to organized books. Payments to subcontractors in the same log also show you who may need a 1099-NEC at year end. Confirm the current reporting threshold with your tax professional.
